The Audio-Visual Analogy (Sieve of Eratosthenes)

The Sieve of Eratosthenes application translates prime factorization and number theory into a sensory, polyphonic experience.

1. Primes as Atomic Tones

In number theory, the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic states that every integer greater than 1 is either prime itself or can be uniquely factored into a product of prime numbers.

In this application:

  • Each prime number discovered is assigned a unique atomic musical pitch derived from the natural harmonic overtone series:

    • 2 = C3 (130.81 Hz—the fundamental bass anchor)

    • 3 = G3 (196.00 Hz—the perfect fifth)

    • 5 = E4 (329.63 Hz—the major third)

    • 7 = B♭4 (466.16 Hz—the harmonic seventh)

    • 11 = F♯5 (739.99 Hz—the augmented fourth)

    • 13 = A5 (880.00 Hz—the natural sixth)

  • Silent on Discovery: When a prime number is first encountered in the sequence, it remains silent. Its tone sounds only when it appears as a factor in subsequent composite numbers.

2. Composites as Harmonic Chords

When the sieve lands on a composite integer, it sounds all of its prime factors simultaneously as a musical chord:

  • 2 anchors the bass (C3C_3).

  • 3 introduces the fifth (G3G_3).

  • 6 = 2×3 sounds an open fifth power chord (C3+G3C_3 + G_3).

  • 5 introduces the warm major third (E4E_4).

  • 30 = 2×3×5 resolves into a full C Major triad (C3+E4+G3C_3 + E_4 + G_3).

3. Prime Powers as Octave Harmonics

When a number contains repeated factors of the same prime:

  • 4 = 2² adds an upper octave harmonic (C4C_4).

  • 8 = 2³ layers the next higher octave (C5C_5).

  • 12 = 2²×3 layers both the bass root, upper octave, and fifth (C3+C4+G3C_3 + C_4 + G_3).

Players can toggle the Harmonics checkbox in the top app bar to switch between rich multi-octave harmonic layering and single fundamental notes.

4. Continuous Proximity Waves

The dashboard at the bottom tracks how close the current fractional playhead tt is to a multiple of each prime pp. It computes a continuous triangle wave metric:

f(t) = | 2 × ((speed × t) mod p) / p - 1.0 |

  • Peaks at 1.0: The moment a multiple arrives (e.g. at every even number for p=2p=2, or every third number for p=3p=3).

  • Troughs at 0.0: Exactly halfway between multiples.

  • Predicting Primes: If all proximity waves are well below 100%, the upcoming number has no existing factors—meaning it must be prime!
